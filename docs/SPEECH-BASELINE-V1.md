# Speech accuracy evaluation — corpus v1

**Corpus:** `speech-command-accuracy` v1, 48 utterances, licence CC0-1.0. **Hypotheses from:** `hand-authored-synthetic`.
**Headline:** word error rate 9.7% and command accuracy 79.2% over 48 utterances.

- Word error rate = (substitutions + deletions + insertions) / reference words, corpus-level per condition after lowercasing and stripping punctuation.
- Command accuracy = the heard transcript routes to the expected tool with the expected arguments, compared after normalising number words and plurals, so a cosmetic word error is not scored as a broken command. `intent: null` means no tool was correct. Measured on the hypotheses.
- The same measure on the reference transcripts. It should be 1.0; anything less is a corpus expectation that the router cannot satisfy, and is listed under corpusErrors.
- Every value carries its sample count. A condition under the minimum reports withheld rather than a percentage.

## What was measured, and what was not

- **Measured:** Word error rate and command accuracy over a text corpus, scored by the shipped deterministic router. Deterministic and offline: no model, no audio, no network.
- **Not measured:** No physical microphone, no acoustic model, no recording of a real person, and no hardware acceptance. The hypotheses are authored text, so the WER measures the fixture; the reference layer measures this checkout's router.

## Corpus expectations

Every reference transcript routes as the corpus expects.

This is not hardware acceptance. There is no microphone, no acoustic model and no recording of a real person in this corpus, so these numbers cannot say how well the product hears you in a kitchen. They say what the shipped router does with a transcript, and how far a plausible transcript drifts from the words that were said.
